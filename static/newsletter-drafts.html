Newsletter Drafts

A newsletter is an email you send to readers who signed up to hear from you. It's one of the best ways to sell books, but staring at a blank "compose" window is hard. This automation drafts your newsletter locally, so you always have something to work from.

What it does

scripts/draft_newsletter.py writes a newsletter draft Markdown file from a book title, an update paragraph, and a few lines you choose. It never sends email — it just gives you a draft to open in any text editor and copy into your newsletter tool (MailerLite, Substack, ConvertKit, or your own email app).

Step by step

  1. Try it in dry-run mode. Open your terminal in this folder and run:

    python3 scripts/draft_newsletter.py \
  --book "The Massive AI Manual" \
  --update "Chapter 12 is finished, and the cover proof came back today." \
  --intro "I hope you're having a good week." \
  --cta "Pre-order here: https://example.com/book"

    You'll see the full draft printed on screen. Nothing is saved yet.

  2. Write the draft to a file. When you're happy with it, run with --apply:

    python3 scripts/draft_newsletter.py \
  --book "The Massive AI Manual" \
  --update "Chapter 12 is finished, and the cover proof came back today." \
  --first-name "Reader" \
  --apply

    This creates drafts/newsletter-draft.md. Open it in any text editor, tweak the wording, then copy it into your newsletter tool.

  3. Personalize and send yourself. Copy the draft into MailerLite, Substack, ConvertKit, or your email app. Replace [your book link] with your real link. Send it to yourself first to check how it looks, then send to your list.

Options you can use

OptionWhat it doesExample
--bookBook title to feature--book "My Book"
--updateWhat's new (required)--update "New cover reveal!"
--first-nameName in the greeting--first-name "Alex"
--introOpening paragraph--intro "Hi there,"
--ctaCall to action / link line--cta "Get it: https://example.com"
--outWhere to write the draft--out drafts/october.md
--applyActually write the file(no value needed)

No-code alternative: keep a "newsletter template" document in your notes app with placeholder sections — greeting, what's new, book link, closing — and fill it in each time you send. The script just automates that into a file.
